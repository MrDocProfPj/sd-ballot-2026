# 2026 candidate-race research for downtown Sioux Falls voters

Research date: November 1, 2026  
Election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026  
Geographic focus: downtown Sioux Falls, Minnehaha County

This is a nonpartisan research brief, not an endorsement. It covers candidate races and voting logistics. It deliberately does not cover ballot measures, because those are assigned to a separate researcher.

## Quick takeaways

- The ordinary downtown Sioux Falls address is in South Dakota Legislative District 15. Districts 9, 10, 11, and 14 are nearby, but the official boundary description and map do not put the downtown core in them. [Official boundary description](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/districtBoundaryDescription.php) [Official Sioux Falls district map](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/SiouxFallsLegislativeDistrictsandVotingPrecincts_UA.pdf) People near an edge should enter their exact address in the state's [Voter Information Portal](https://vip.sdsos.gov/VIPLogin.aspx) rather than rely on a neighborhood label.
- The certified U.S. Senate ballot has Republican Mike Rounds and independent Brian Bengs. Democratic nominee Julian Beaudion withdrew on August 4, and no Libertarian Senate candidate appears on the Secretary of State's certified list. [Official 2026 general-election candidate list](https://vip.sdsos.gov/candidatelist.aspx?eid=774)
- Minnehaha County commissioners are elected countywide, not from geographic commissioner districts. Every downtown voter gets the same at-large commission race and may vote for up to three candidates. [Official candidate list](https://vip.sdsos.gov/candidatelist.aspx?eid=774)
- Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Central Time. Voters who do not have an accepted photo ID may sign a personal identification affidavit and cast a regular ballot. [South Dakota voting rules](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/voting/default.aspx)

## What should appear on a downtown ballot

The authoritative statewide source is the South Dakota Secretary of State's [2026 general-election candidate list](https://vip.sdsos.gov/candidatelist.aspx?eid=774). ### Courts

No South Dakota Supreme Court retention question and no Second Judicial Circuit judge race was found in the Secretary of State's certified 2026 candidate list. The Second Circuit includes Minnehaha and Lincoln counties, so this is the circuit relevant to Sioux Falls. [Official candidate list](https://vip.sdsos.gov/candidatelist.aspx?eid=774)

This negative finding deserves a caution: Minnehaha County does not appear to have posted one public countywide sample-ballot PDF that can be checked without entering an address. Justice Janine Kern retired in December 2025, and Robert Gusinsky was appointed to replace her in January 2026; South Dakota's rules ordinarily put an appointed justice before voters after three years, which points to 2028 rather than 2026. [Kern retirement notice](https://ujs.sd.gov/ujs-news/south-dakota-supreme-court-justice-janine-m-kern-announces-retirement/) [UJS judicial-election rules](https://ujs.sd.gov/judicial-elections-committee/) [UJS FY2026 annual report](https://ujs.sd.gov/media/onilgw3t/ujsfy2026annualreport.pdf)

**Confidence:** high that there is no statewide Supreme Court retention question on the certified list; moderate-to-high that there is no address-specific Second Circuit contest. Voters should use the [Voter Information Portal](https://vip.sdsos.gov/VIPLogin.aspx) for the final ballot image.

## Major statewide races and candidates

### Governor and lieutenant governor

#### Larry Rhoden and Tony Venhuizen, Republican

Larry Rhoden is a Union Center-area rancher, former National Guard member, and longtime state legislator. He served as lieutenant governor from 2019 until becoming governor in early 2025 after Kristi Noem joined the federal Cabinet. His campaign and public statements emphasize property-tax relief, public safety, low taxes, gun rights, opposition to abortion, rural health, and expanding defense manufacturing; as governor, he also signed the 2025 law barring eminent domain for carbon-dioxide pipelines. [Official state biography](https://sdsos.gov/general-information/assets/bluebook-docs/2025BlueBook.pdf) [AP campaign profile](https://apnews.com/article/9ae7c9443328c9c257d3f5da798a0ca5) [Dakota News Now interview](https://www.dakotanewsnow.com/2026/09/16/inside-race-dan-ahlers-larry-rhoden-talk-key-issues-with-dakota-news-now/) [AP carbon-pipeline report](https://apnews.com/article/3160bc081070d0d1e0b9405d6ddde138)

Tony Venhuizen, an attorney from Armour who lives in Sioux Falls, became lieutenant governor in January 2025. He previously represented District 13 in the state House, served on the House Appropriations Committee, and worked for ten years in governors' offices, including as chief of staff to Dennis Daugaard and Kristi Noem. [Official state biography](https://sdsos.gov/general-information/assets/bluebook-docs/2025BlueBook.pdf) [Official swearing-in announcement](https://news.sd.gov/news?id=news_kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0043413)

Rhoden's most visible recent record includes securing legislative approval for a roughly $650 million replacement for the aging state penitentiary and proposing a local-option half-cent sales tax that counties could use to replace some property-tax revenue. Supporters present those actions as solutions to long-running prison and property-tax problems; critics question the prison price and whether shifting from property tax to sales tax produces enough relief for lower-income households. [AP governor profile](https://apnews.com/article/9ae7c9443328c9c257d3f5da798a0ca5) [Governor's property-tax proposal](https://news.sd.gov/news?id=news_kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0045970)

#### Dan Ahlers and Steven McCleerey, Democratic

Dan Ahlers is a Dell Rapids small-business owner and former state representative and senator. His campaign emphasizes rural main streets, roads and broadband, family farms, rural health and long-term care, mental-health services, public education, workforce pay, small businesses, and more transparent government; he argues that greater state support for schools can reduce pressure on local property taxes. [Ahlers biography](https://www.danahlers.com/about) [Ahlers issues](https://www.danahlers.com/about-3) [Brookings Radio debate report](https://www.brookingsradio.com/news/gov-candidates-debate-affordability-education/)

Steven McCleerey is a Sisseton crop and livestock farmer who represented District 1 in the state House from 2015 through 2020. He also served about two decades on a local hospital board, and his selection adds agricultural, rural-health, and legislative experience to the ticket. [South Dakota Searchlight profile](https://southdakotasearchlight.com/2026/08/12/democratic-nominee-for-lieutenant-governor-brings-legislative-and-agricultural-experience-to-race/) [Argus Leader profile republished by AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/sisseton-farmer-former-state-legislator-203234000.html)

The central difference is the role of state government. Rhoden and Venhuizen defend the incumbent Republican administration and emphasize low taxes, public safety, limited government, and targeted economic development. Ahlers and McCleerey argue for more state investment in education, health care, infrastructure, and rural communities, with stronger transparency and oversight. [Rhoden interview](https://www.dakotanewsnow.com/2026/09/16/inside-race-dan-ahlers-larry-rhoden-talk-key-issues-with-dakota-news-now/) [Ahlers issues](https://www.danahlers.com/about-3)

#### Primary result that matters

The Republican nomination required two elections. In the June 2 primary, Toby Doeden led with about 30.6 percent, Rhoden had about 25.2 percent, U.S. Rep. Dusty Johnson had about 23.4 percent, and state House Speaker Jon Hansen had about 20.8 percent; because nobody reached the 35 percent threshold, Doeden and Rhoden advanced to a runoff. [Certified runoff-result summary](https://ballotpedia.org/July_28%2C_2026%2C_election_results) Ahlers was unopposed for the Democratic nomination. ### U.S. Senate

#### Mike Rounds, Republican

Mike Rounds is a former insurance and real-estate business owner who served in the state Senate and as governor from 2003 to 2011; he has served in the U.S. Senate since 2015. His campaign emphasizes national security, veterans, agriculture, cybersecurity, gun rights, an "all of the above" energy policy, local control of education, opposition to abortion except when the mother's life is at risk, and support for President Trump's agenda. [Rounds campaign issues](https://roundsforsenate.com/issues/) [Official Senate website](https://www.rounds.senate.gov/)

Rounds offers voters seniority and an established conservative voting record. He won the Republican primary over Justin McNeal with roughly 76 percent of the vote. [South Dakota News Watch primary guide](https://www.sdnewswatch.org/2026-primary-election-top-races/) [Official primary results](https://electionresults.sd.gov/)

#### Brian Bengs, independent

Brian Bengs is an attorney, educator, retired Air Force lieutenant colonel, and former Navy enlistee and national-park ranger who has lived in South Dakota since 2016. His campaign emphasizes reducing the influence of large donors, congressional term limits, health-care access and affordability, and giving local communities an opt-out before new artificial-intelligence data centers are built; he also opposes continued U.S. involvement in the Iran war. [Bengs campaign biography and issues](https://bengsforsouthdakota.com/) [KOTA interview](https://www.kotatv.com/2026/07/14/special-interview-independent-senate-candidate-brian-bengs-outlines-campaign-priorities-south-dakota-race/)

Bengs ran as a Democrat against John Thune in 2022 and received about 26 percent of the vote, but he is running as an independent in 2026. The main contrast is between Rounds's incumbent Republican record and alignment with national conservative policy, and Bengs's argument that an independent can reduce partisanship and the power of money in politics. [AP primary preview](https://apnews.com/article/945fbd3a0c1610da1a93bf4827f0909c) [Rounds issues](https://roundsforsenate.com/issues/) [Bengs campaign](https://bengsforsouthdakota.com/)

## Downtown legislative district

### Which district covers downtown?

District 15 covers the downtown core. Minnehaha County's legal description places District 15 east of Interstate 29, with its southern downtown boundary generally following 14th Street from Cliff Avenue to Minnesota Avenue, then jogging south to 18th Street before continuing west to Interstate 29; the district extends north through central and north-central Sioux Falls. [Official district boundary description](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/districtBoundaryDescription.php) [Official 2026 Sioux Falls legislative-district map](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/SiouxFallsLegislativeDistrictsandVotingPrecincts_UA.pdf)

District 10 is immediately east or southeast of parts of central Sioux Falls, District 14 is farther southeast, District 11 is west of Interstate 29 and south of 12th Street, and District 9 is west or northwest of Interstate 29. Those four districts do not cover the conventional downtown core. Because a street-level boundary can divide nearby blocks, anyone who uses "downtown" loosely or lives near 14th Street, Cliff Avenue, Minnesota Avenue, or Interstate 29 should verify the exact address in the [Voter Information Portal](https://vip.sdsos.gov/VIPLogin.aspx).

### District 15 State House

Voters may choose up to two candidates. The certified field is Andre Zachariahs and Joni Tschetter, Republicans, and Kadyn Wittman and June CopperStad, Democrats. [Official candidate list](https://vip.sdsos.gov/candidatelist.aspx?eid=774)

## Big issues running through the races

### Property taxes and affordability

Property-tax relief is one of the clearest state-level dividing lines. Rhoden proposes allowing a county half-cent sales tax to replace some county property-tax revenue; Ahlers argues the state should take on more education costs so local districts need less property-tax revenue. District 15 candidates also cite property taxes, housing, wages, child care, and everyday costs, but disagree about whether the best response is direct public support, tax shifts, spending restraint, or local action. [Rhoden property-tax proposal](https://news.sd.gov/news?id=news_kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0045970) [Ahlers debate report](https://www.brookingsradio.com/news/gov-candidates-debate-affordability-education/) [Sioux Falls Simplified District 15 guide](https://www.sfsimplified.com/meet-the-dist-15-folks-who-want-to-represent-you-in-pierre/)

### Prisons and public safety

Rhoden points to the approved penitentiary replacement as a major accomplishment, while the project's roughly $650 million cost and location continue to make prison policy an issue. District 15 contains the existing state penitentiary area, which makes corrections, neighborhood effects, reentry, homelessness, and public safety especially local concerns. [AP governor profile](https://apnews.com/article/9ae7c9443328c9c257d3f5da798a0ca5) [District 15 boundary description](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/districtBoundaryDescription.php) [KELO District 15 candidate report](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/running-house-district-15-192459000.html)

### Medicaid and health care

Health-care access, rural hospitals, Medicaid reimbursement, and the future of Medicaid expansion divide the broader party agendas. The Democratic platform calls for fully funding Medicaid, higher provider reimbursement, universal affordable coverage, and more rural and behavioral-health capacity; the Republican platform favors private-market medicine, price transparency, health savings, and opposition to government-run universal coverage. Individual candidates vary, so those party positions should not be treated as a substitute for a candidate's own statement. [SD Democratic platform](https://www.sddp.org/sddp-platform) [SD Republican platform](https://www.sdgop.com/platform/)

### Carbon pipelines, utilities, and eminent domain

Landowner rights remain prominent after Rhoden signed a 2025 law barring the use of eminent domain for carbon-dioxide pipelines. Gronli likewise says private projects should not take land through eminent domain, while the PUC race matters because the commission decides utility and some pipeline cases. [AP carbon-pipeline report](https://apnews.com/article/3160bc081070d0d1e0b9405d6ddde138) [Gronli issues](https://www.nikkigforsd.com/services-8) [Secretary of State offices-to-be-filled page](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/upcoming-elections/general-information/2026-offices-to-be-filled.aspx)

### Data centers and local control

Large data centers have become a cross-party concern involving electricity, water, land use, tax incentives, and local authority. District 15 candidates range from CopperStad's opposition to hyperscale projects, through Wittman's call for regulation and Tschetter's proposed two-year pause, to Zachariahs's emphasis on responsible development and local control; Bengs also wants communities to have an opt-out before new AI data centers are built. [KELO District 15 candidate report](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/running-house-district-15-192459000.html) [Bengs interview](https://www.kotatv.com/2026/07/14/special-interview-independent-senate-candidate-brian-bengs-outlines-campaign-priorities-south-dakota-race/)

## Party platforms in brief

These are party-level positions, not a guarantee that every nominee agrees with every plank.

### South Dakota Republican Party

The 2026 Republican platform emphasizes individual liberty, limited government, personal responsibility, private property, lower taxes and spending, gun rights, opposition to abortion, traditional marriage, parental authority and local control in education, school choice, border enforcement, election integrity, law enforcement, and domestic energy production. On health care, it favors private-market medicine and transparency and opposes a government-run universal system. [Official 2026 SDGOP platform](https://www.sdgop.com/platform/)

### South Dakota Democratic Party

The platform adopted June 6, 2026 emphasizes family farms and local agricultural control; fully funded public education and competitive teacher pay; universal, affordable health care and full Medicaid funding; child care, paid parental leave, housing, and behavioral-health services; fairer taxation, including repeal of the grocery sales tax; infrastructure and broadband; labor rights; tribal sovereignty and treaty obligations; and stronger citizen participation and government accountability. [Official 2026 SDDP platform](https://www.sddp.org/sddp-platform)

## Voting logistics for Minnehaha County

### Poll hours

Polls are open Tuesday, November 3, from **7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time**. Sioux Falls is in the Central Time Zone. Anyone in line by 7 p.m. is entitled to vote. [South Dakota voting information](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/voting/default.aspx) [Minnehaha County election information](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/electionInfo/electionInfo.php)

### Find the correct polling place and sample ballot

Use the state's [Voter Information Portal](https://vip.sdsos.gov/VIPLogin.aspx), which looks up registration, polling place, precinct, districts, and sample ballot by voter information. Minnehaha County also has a [precinct and polling-place page](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/election/precinct_info/precinct_info.php). Checking the address is important because nearby blocks can be in different legislative or precinct boundaries.

### Photo identification

Accepted photo ID includes:

- a South Dakota driver's license or non-driver ID;
- a U.S. government photo ID, including a passport;
- a U.S. Armed Forces photo ID;
- a current student photo ID from a South Dakota high school or accredited South Dakota college, university, or technical school; or
- a tribal photo ID.

A voter who cannot present one of these may sign a **personal identification affidavit** under penalty of perjury and then cast a regular ballot. Lack of photo ID by itself does not mean the voter must leave without voting. [Official South Dakota voter-ID rules](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/voting/default.aspx)

### Registration

The registration deadline for this election was October 19, 2026. South Dakota does **not** offer Election Day or same-day voter registration, so an unregistered person cannot register at the polls on November 3. [Official 2026 election calendar](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/upcoming-elections/general-information/default.aspx) [Secretary of State FAQ](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/upcoming-elections/Myths-vs-Facts-and-FAQS/faqs.aspx)

### In-person absentee voting

In-person absentee voting remains available through **Monday, November 2**, the day before the election. Minnehaha County lists weekday hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. at the County Administration Building, 415 N. Dakota Ave.; its election-information page directs absentee voters to the third floor. [Minnehaha County election information](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/electionInfo/electionInfo.php) State law and guidance allow an in-person absentee application through 5 p.m. on the day before the election. [Secretary of State absentee-voting guide](https://sdsos.gov/elections-voting/voting/absentee-voting.aspx)

Because county web pages can change room assignments, a voter going on November 2 should call the Auditor at 605-367-4220 if uncertain about the floor or entrance. [Minnehaha County Auditor](https://minnehahacounty.gov/dept/au/au.php)

### Returning an absentee ballot

A completed absentee ballot must be received by the county election official early enough to be delivered to the voter's precinct before polls close at **7 p.m. local time on November 3**. A postmark is not enough. With Election Day two days away, hand delivery to the Auditor is the safer option than ordinary mail. [Secretary of State absentee-voting guide](https://sdsos.gov/elections-voting/voting/absentee-voting.aspx) [Secretary of State FAQ](https://www.sdsos.gov/elections-voting/upcoming-elections/Myths-vs-Facts-and-FAQS/faqs.aspx)

### No straight-ticket option

South Dakota does not have a one-mark straight-party option. A voter must mark each candidate race separately and may mix parties or choose independents. The state eliminated straight-ticket voting in 1997. [1997 South Dakota legislation](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/Bill/73874.pdf?Year=1997) [National Conference of State Legislatures summary](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/straight-ticket-voting)
