# National context for South Dakota's 2026 ballot

**Current through Nov. 2, 2026.** Counts, polling and race ratings can change through Election Day. Each factual bullet links directly to its source.

## 1. Balance of power now

- **House:** 218 Republicans, 214 Democrats, 1 independent and 2 vacancies (433 sitting voting members). Independent Kevin Kiley caucuses with Republicans, so the practical governing split is 219–214. The vacancies are TX-23 (formerly Republican) and FL-20 (formerly Democratic). [House Clerk/current count](https://clerk.house.gov/) · [House Clerk/vacancies](https://clerk.house.gov/Members/ViewVacancies) · [AP on Kiley](https://apnews.com/article/cf984d5b264563dc2d43aacbf4da7cc1)
- **House control in this election:** All 435 seats are on the ballot and 218 is a majority if every seat is filled. Republicans already control the chamber. Democrats need to win **218 seats**—four more than the 214 Democrats serving now, or a **net gain of 3** compared with the 2024 election result (when 215 Democrats were elected). [Ballotpedia/2026 House](https://ballotpedia.org/United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections,_2026)
- **House leader:** Speaker **Mike Johnson (R-Louisiana)**; Hakeem Jeffries (D-New York) is minority leader. [House Clerk/leadership](https://clerk.house.gov/Members/ViewLeadership)
- **Senate:** 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats and 2 independents; both independents caucus with Democrats, making the working split 53–47. There are no vacancies. [U.S. Senate/party division](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/party-division.htm)
- **Senate control in this election:** 35 seats are on the ballot—33 regularly scheduled Class II seats plus 2 special elections; 22 are Republican-held and 13 Democratic-held. Republicans already control the chamber. Democrats need a **net gain of 4** to reach 51; Republicans can lose **3 net seats** and still organize a 50–50 Senate because Vice President JD Vance can break a tie. [Ballotpedia/2026 Senate](https://ballotpedia.org/United_States_Senate_elections%2C_2026) · [Constitution/vice-president tie vote](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/article-1/section-3/)
- **Senate leader:** South Dakota Sen. **John Thune is still Senate majority leader**; Chuck Schumer (D-New York) is minority leader. [U.S. Senate/majority and minority leaders](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/majority-minority-leaders.htm)

## 2. What control would change nationally

- **If Republicans keep both chambers:** Trump would retain the clearest path for party-line tax, spending and regulatory legislation and for Senate confirmation of judges and executive nominees. This would not guarantee passage: most Senate legislation still needs 60 votes to end debate, while budget reconciliation has special majority-vote rules. [Senate/filibuster and cloture](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/filibusters-cloture.htm) · [CRS/reconciliation](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44058)
- **If Democrats win only the House:** They could block House passage of Republican bills, set the House agenda, chair committees, issue committee subpoenas and open investigations. A Democratic House could impeach by simple majority, but removal would require a two-thirds Senate conviction; a Republican Senate would keep control of confirmations. [Reuters/scenarios](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/the-midterm-election-outcomes-that-could-shape-trumps-final-two-years-4967002) · [Senate/impeachment](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/impeachment.htm)
- **If Democrats win only the Senate:** They could control Senate committees and the floor and block or delay Trump’s judicial, Cabinet and ambassador nominees. A Republican House could still block Democratic legislation. [Senate/powers and procedures](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures.htm) · [Reuters/scenarios](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/the-midterm-election-outcomes-that-could-shape-trumps-final-two-years-4967002)
- **If Democrats win both chambers:** They could run investigations in both chambers and send their own bills to Trump, but he could veto them; overriding a veto requires two-thirds of both chambers. Most Senate legislation would also remain subject to the 60-vote cloture threshold. [White House/legislative branch](https://www.whitehouse.gov/government/legislative-branch/) · [Senate/cloture](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/filibusters-cloture.htm)
- **Budget and shutdown leverage:** Annual funding must pass both chambers and be signed by the president. Divided control would give each party a veto point and could produce more bargaining over shutdown deadlines and the debt limit; either side could also compromise and pass bipartisan funding. [CRS/funding gaps](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS20348.html) · [Reuters/scenarios](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/the-midterm-election-outcomes-that-could-shape-trumps-final-two-years-4967002)
- **“Impeachment talk” versus removal:** Democratic candidates and lawmakers have discussed investigations and possible impeachment, but even a House impeachment would not itself remove Trump. Conviction requires two-thirds of senators present, far beyond any plausible 2026 Democratic majority. [Senate/impeachment process](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/impeachment.htm) · [Reuters/scenarios](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/the-midterm-election-outcomes-that-could-shape-trumps-final-two-years-4967002)

## 3. Trump second-term agenda voters are reacting to

### 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)

- **Status:** Trump signed the budget-reconciliation law on **July 4, 2025** (Public Law 119-21). It extended or made permanent many 2017 individual tax provisions and added temporary deductions for tips, overtime and interest on some U.S.-made vehicle loans. CBO estimated it would add about **$3.4 trillion** to deficits through 2034. [GovInfo/law](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-119publ21) · [AP/contents and CBO estimate](https://apnews.com/article/6a58710651382dcce5083b31ac985042)
- **Medicaid:** The law requires many Medicaid-expansion adults ages 19–64 to document **80 hours a month** of work, community service, education or qualifying activity, with exemptions. States must begin no later than **Jan. 1, 2027**. CBO's enacted-law estimate says the Medicaid chapter reduces federal spending by **$886.8 billion** over 2025–34 and leaves **7.5 million** more people uninsured in 2034; all health provisions together were estimated to increase the uninsured population by about **10 million**. [CBO/enacted Medicaid estimate](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61837) · [KFF/Oct. 21, 2026 update](https://www.kff.org/elections/midterm-election-update-medicaid-changes/)
- **SNAP food aid:** The law broadened work rules, narrowed some waiver authority, raised states’ share of administrative costs from 50% to 75%, and beginning in FY2028 makes states with payment-error rates of 6% or more pay a share of benefits. CBO estimated roughly **$187 billion** in nutrition-program reductions over 10 years. [CRS/SNAP](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48552.html) · [CRS/reconciliation summary](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48755.html)
- **What the sides argue:** Republicans describe the law as preventing a large tax increase, rewarding work and reducing fraud; Democrats emphasize projected coverage and food-aid losses and argue that the largest tax benefits go to higher-income households. Those are competing policy arguments, not settled factual judgments. [AP/law and midterm arguments](https://apnews.com/article/4127f86db9aa557ae9316913aed7d80e) · [CBO/distributional effects](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61535)

### Tariffs, farm exports and prices

- **Court status:** On **Feb. 20, 2026**, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act did not authorize Trump’s broad emergency tariffs. The ruling invalidated tariffs imposed under IEEPA, not every tariff imposed under other trade statutes; later product and country agreements continued to change the active schedule. [Supreme Court/opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf) · [AP/September 2026 tariff changes](https://apnews.com/article/f47781187c501e7c4f10a686fb2572e2)
- **Agriculture:** China’s retaliation in 2025 produced a near-boycott of U.S. agricultural goods, including soybeans. A late-2025 U.S.–China deal revived some buying, but **soybeans were excluded** from the September 2026 list of products receiving new tariff cuts. [AP/farm impact, Nov. 1, 2026](https://apnews.com/article/113757ded4631a2debfa184a36c4d85e) · [AP/September tariff list](https://apnews.com/article/f47781187c501e7c4f10a686fb2572e2)
- **Prices require nuance:** AP reported that the 2025 tariff fight and loss of the China market pushed soybean prices lower. Prices later recovered: USDA-derived South Dakota data show **$12.20/bushel in September 2026**, up from **$9.41 in September 2025**. That recovery does not by itself establish which policy caused it; crop supply, energy, currency and global demand also move prices. [AP/soybean pressure](https://apnews.com/article/5731e2d79ce125bfa0a667a862dbe35e) · [USDA-derived South Dakota price series](https://brookingsview.com/farm-report/)
- **Current farm response:** USDA announced more than **$15 billion** in farm payments and initiatives shortly before the election, in addition to $13.8 billion in previously planned safety-net payments for the 2025 crop. The administration says tariffs protect U.S. producers and create bargaining leverage; critics and affected growers point to retaliatory market losses and higher fuel, fertilizer and equipment costs. [AP/Nov. 1 farm aid and arguments](https://apnews.com/article/113757ded4631a2debfa184a36c4d85e)

### Immigration enforcement

- The administration sharply expanded detention, border spending and removal efforts; the OBBBA provided roughly **$170 billion** for immigration enforcement and border security. Migration Policy Institute estimated **980,000 deportations** from Jan. 20, 2025 through July 31, 2026, with the FY2026 pace about 660,000 a year—higher than recent administrations but below Trump’s stated 1-million-a-year goal. [CBO/immigration provisions](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105/) · [Migration Policy Institute/Oct. 21, 2026](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/press-release/deportations-during-first-18-months-trump)
- MPI estimated **69%** of those deported were intercepted at the border and **20%** of all deportees had a criminal conviction. Supporters emphasize border control, removal of unauthorized immigrants and public safety; critics emphasize due process, family and workplace disruption, and the removal of people without criminal convictions. [Migration Policy Institute](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/press-release/deportations-during-first-18-months-trump)

### Federal workforce cuts and DOGE

- GAO found the workforce at 22 major agencies fell by nearly **256,000 employees, or more than 11%**, from December 2024 to January 2026 (2,268,585 to 2,012,614). DOGE stopped operating as a centralized entity in late 2025, while agencies continued workforce-reduction plans. [GAO/federal workforce](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108583) · [Reuters/DOGE status](https://www.investing.com/news/politics-news/us-federal-workforce-drops-to-lowest-level-in-at-least-a-decade-4438066)
- The administration says the cuts reduce waste and overhead. Federal unions, former officials and other critics say rushed cuts removed expertise and weakened services; the long-term savings and service effects remain disputed. [AP/DOGE one-year assessment](https://apnews.com/article/ed82cbe516fbc527b0d8392e7b8098dc) · [GAO/workforce evidence](https://www.gao.gov/federal-workforce)

### Shutdowns and budget fights

- There were three FY2026 funding gaps: a government-wide gap from **Oct. 1–Nov. 12, 2025 (42 full days)**; a six-appropriations-bill gap beginning **Jan. 31, 2026 (3 full days)**; and a DHS-only gap from **Feb. 14–Apr. 30, 2026 (75 full days)**. CRS's DHS table counts the inclusive calendar dates as 43, 3 and 76; Border Patrol and ICE annual appropriations remained unresolved until June 10, although multi-year reconciliation money kept many functions operating. [CRS/all federal funding gaps](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS20348.html) · [CRS/DHS lapse table](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49105.html)
- Trump signed a continuing resolution on **Sept. 2, 2026** funding the government through **Dec. 11**, avoiding an Oct. 1 pre-election shutdown but leaving another deadline for the post-election Congress. [Reuters/Sept. 2 funding law](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/trump-signs-bill-to-avert-government-shutdown-before-midterm-elections-4886940)

### ACA health insurance

- Enhanced Affordable Care Act premium tax credits expired after **Dec. 31, 2025**; that expiration was scheduled separately and was **not created by the OBBBA**. KFF found the average 2026 premium payment among Marketplace enrollees rose **58%, from $113 to $178 a month**, while the average deductible rose **37%, to $3,786**. [KFF/2026 enrollment, premiums and deductibles](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/) · [FactCheck.org/OBBBA distinction](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-not-yet-driving-aca-medicaid-declines/)
- KFF reported effectuated Marketplace enrollment fell from **21.8 million in 2025 to 19.2 million in February 2026**; by June, total individual-market enrollment (about nine-tenths of it Marketplace coverage) was down **18% year over year, to 20.5 million**. Republicans opposing an enhanced-credit extension cite cost, insurer pricing and improper enrollment; Democrats favoring renewal cite higher premiums and coverage losses. [KFF/Oct. 6 Marketplace update](https://www.kff.org/elections/midterm-election-update-the-affordable-care-act-marketplaces/) · [KFF/Oct. 14 midyear enrollment](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/individual-market-enrollment-continues-to-fall-through-mid-2026/)

### Other dominant 2026 issues

- **Cost of living and Iran:** AP’s Election Day-eve coverage identifies persistent inflation, gasoline prices and the Iran war as major forces in the midterms. Trump argues the war prevented an Iranian nuclear weapon and points to recent oil-price declines; Democrats and some Republicans argue that the war and tariffs raised household and farm costs. [AP/Nov. 2 midterm coverage](https://apnews.com/article/7dcb866713d99a2f0beaa9c037d09ddd)
- **AI data centers and electricity:** AP estimated five large technology companies planned about **$700 billion** in U.S. data-center spending in 2026. Supporters cite jobs, tax base and competition with China; opponents focus on who pays for new power capacity, plus water and land use. Those disputes became campaign issues in multiple states. [AP/data centers and midterms](https://www.ap.org/news-highlights/elections/2026/heres-all-the-ways-that-data-center-controversies-have-transformed-the-midterm-elections/)

## 5. South Dakota-specific national ties

- **Kristi Noem correction:** Noem served as DHS secretary from **Jan. 25, 2025, until Trump removed her on March 5, 2026**; she is **not the current DHS secretary**. Former Oklahoma Sen. Markwayne Mullin was confirmed and sworn in March 24. Noem’s later special-envoy appointment expired in September; as of Oct. 16 she was a senior adviser to Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau on “Shield of the Americas.” [AP/Noem’s removal](https://apnews.com/article/38c583b3cef97b4ef60d84b8f8b5961a) · [White House/Mullin](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/03/secretary-markwayne-mullin-is-ready-to-deliver-on-president-trumps-agenda/) · [CBS/Noem’s current role](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/kristi-noem-no-longer-trump-envoy-shield-of-the-americas/)
- **Medicaid enrollment and work rules:** The latest CMS-based count available was **138,342 South Dakotans in July 2026**—123,938 in Medicaid and 14,404 in CHIP. Separately, South Dakota DSS counted **29,504 expansion enrollees** at the end of 2025 and estimated **1,213 (about 4%)** could lose coverage under the federal work rule in 2027 “if we do nothing”; state officials said exemptions and outreach could reduce that number. The 1,213 estimate covers the work rule, not every OBBBA Medicaid provision. [KFF/CMS monthly enrollment](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-indicator/medicaid-and-chip-monthly-enrollment/) · [South Dakota Searchlight/DSS estimate](https://southdakotasearchlight.com/2026/04/26/1213-south-dakotans-could-lose-medicaid-as-federal-work-requirements-take-effect-department-says/) · [South Dakota DSS/HR1](https://dss.sd.gov/medicaid/HR1.aspx)
- **Rural hospitals and the rural fund:** The OBBBA created a **$50 billion, five-year Rural Health Transformation Program**; CMS awarded South Dakota **$189,477,607 for FY2026**. KFF estimates the law reduces federal Medicaid spending in rural areas nationally by **$137 billion over 10 years**. The figures are not direct offsets: the fund can support many rural-health projects, while Medicaid reductions affect patients and providers. Supporters say the fund will modernize rural care; critics say it is temporary and smaller than the projected rural Medicaid reduction. [CMS/FY2026 state awards](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-announces-50-billion-awards-strengthen-rural-health-all-50-states) · [KFF/rural effects](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/how-might-federal-medicaid-cuts-in-the-enacted-reconciliation-package-affect-rural-areas/)
- **Soybeans:** South Dakota has about **5,000 soybean producers**, normally grows about **230 million bushels a year**, and historically sends about **30%** of its crop to China. State growers sold none to China during the early 2025 tariff shock, making national trade policy unusually important locally. [South Dakota News Watch/tariffs and agriculture](https://www.sdnewswatch.org/tariffs-trade-south-dakota-agriculture/)
- **Current farm picture:** South Dakota’s September 2026 average soybean price was $12.20/bushel, but China still excluded U.S. soybeans from its September tariff-relief list, and AP reported elevated diesel and fertilizer costs. The evidence therefore supports both a price recovery from 2025 and continuing export/input-cost risk. [USDA-derived state prices](https://brookingsview.com/farm-report/) · [AP/China list](https://apnews.com/article/f47781187c501e7c4f10a686fb2572e2) · [AP/farm costs](https://apnews.com/article/113757ded4631a2debfa184a36c4d85e)
- **Ellsworth AFB/B-21:** Ellsworth is the first main operating base for the B-21 Raider. The Air Force activated its B-21 Integration Detachment there on **Sept. 10, 2026**, with the first aircraft planned for **2027**; the base is in a roughly **$2 billion** construction program. The OBBBA provided **$4.5 billion** in FY2026 mandatory funding for B-21 production, linking the national law directly to Ellsworth's future mission. [Air Force Global Strike Command/integration detachment](https://www.afgsc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4595645/unifying-the-raiders-beddown-eighth-air-force-activates-b-21-integration-detach/) · [Air Force/facilities and construction](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4532548/secaf-visits-ellsworth-afb-as-base-reaches-historic-b-21-raider-facilities-mile/) · [CRS/FY2026 weapons funding](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48860.html)
